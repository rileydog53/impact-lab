# Version 0.8 experimental fracture

The assembly solver now starts fracture-enabled bodies on shared FEM nodes. It creates cohesive interfaces and splits vertex connectivity when a face meets the opening or shear criterion. This checkpoint delivers on-demand fracture in the assembly editor. The orientation and secondary-contact studies failed the release gates, so results remain experimental.

## Numerical model

- Constant-strain triangles provide one first Piola stress on each side. The mean resolved traction sets the insertion criterion and initializes the new interface in its current frame. A split requires at least one endpoint to separate into distinct groups of incident triangles. This limits isolated interior nucleation.
- The topology change transfers position and half-step velocity to the new nodes, reallocates lumped mass by incident element, and preserves element state and material attachments. The final half kick uses forces on the new connectivity. The traction-separation law uses zero physical gap at insertion, a finite seed traction, irreversible softening, and equal opening and shear toughness.
- Contact uses the actual new boundary nodes. Each matching node-to-edge feature can retain paired closure while other surfaces on the same fragments collide. The matching feature changes representation only when both its paired closure and its ordinary contact are unloaded. Fully failed interfaces still determine when a sibling gets its own collision domain. That can expose overlap that accumulated inside a previously connected body.
- Candidate paths still follow mesh edges and pay fracture energy per unit of actual edge length. Staggering the block mesh changes the available directions, and both patterns retain horizontal rows. The model has no compressive shear initiation, general same-piece self-contact, or edge-only collision detection. All calculations remain 2D plane strain.

[Pandolfi and Ortiz, adaptive cohesive insertion](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0106318)

## Contact repair and remaining gates

At `923c8f8`, fragment pair 0/247 already had 0.3865 J/m of ordinary contact potential one step before the 28.771 ms rejection. The pair-wide switch had suppressed it. The feature-level repair allows nonmatching contacts to act while matching crack faces still carry closure. In the same fine staggered run, the pair now begins ordinary contact at 27.857 ms, accumulates 0.049962 N·s/m of scalar normal impulse, and reaches 115 ms. The total energy change at the former rejection was −4.3e−6 J/m. Peak energy imbalance was 0.033%, with momentum residuals of 3.64e−14 N·s/m and 1.26e−15 N·s. Peak penetration was 4.83%, above the 2% gate.

The repeated two-pattern, three-resolution study still fails. Receiver contact occurred in 7 of 12 runs and tagged new-face contact occurred in 3. The aligned extra-fine half-step run rejected 15.94% trial overlap at 28.906 ms when its fourth fragment acquired a collision domain. The insertion count stayed at 122 while the spent-face count increased from 23 to 24. Trying collision domains from the moment of node insertion moved the problem earlier. In that trial the same extra-fine run rejected a 0.0631 J/m one-step contact-energy increase at 13.408 ms. The fine staggered case also rejected a step. The trial was reverted. Moving the domain boundary in time is insufficient when already-overlapping surface features enter the contact search.

The notched-bend work discrepancy survives the contact repair. At extra fine, work at matched advance was 0.7634 J/m for aligned and 0.3476 J/m for staggered. The adjacent measured work intervals were disjoint, so interpolation cannot explain the 54.47% difference. Away from the notch-connected crack, fully spent faces accounted for 0.1560 J/m of the difference and partially damaged faces for 0.2482 J/m. Together they explain about 97% of the difference. Work in the spent notch-connected crack differed by only 0.0033 J/m. Peak striker load differed by 13.69% and occurred before measurable fracture dissipation in both patterns. The next controlled bend should isolate the mesh dependence of the loading response before changing the fracture criterion.

The nominal production step remains 2.218 µs for the standard case. A paired standard-case probe measured 0.551 s and 7.38 MiB at `923c8f8`, and 0.558 s and 7.33 MiB with the repair. `ImpactCheck --sibling-contact`, `--fracture`, `--assembly`, and the native assembly smoke passed. The scientific study still exits nonzero.

### Contact at detachment

`ImpactCheck --sliding-contact-patch` exercises a two-triangle patch through node splitting, compressed detachment, opening, and sliding recontact. Paired closure energy stayed constant through detachment and its relative force change was 2.55e−9 across the two load samples. Handoff on the open seam transferred zero penalty energy. Recontact reached 0.10% penetration and fracture work was 0.140000 J/m.

At diagnostic checkpoint `c89b78c`, `ImpactCheck --detachment-contact` exited nonzero. It traced features by element corner and compared collision domains at the same trial positions. In the aligned extra-fine half-step case, both features had negative local gaps for over 3.7 ms before the fourth domain activated at 28.905691 ms. Two proper edge crossings were already present at the preceding accepted positions. At activation the 15.941% matching hit stayed on paired closure. A distinct ordinary hit at 2.312% would introduce 0.008482 J/m and about 250 N/m of normal force, with unchanged paired closure energy at the same positions. The guard rejected the step before those forces could act. An already overlapping feature entering the search explains the jump. The FEM indices were stable at this event, the ordinary hit appeared once, and the ordinary penalty has no stored history to reset.

The repair replaces paired compression and post-detachment sibling penalties with a single geometric potential for overlapping triangles of a fracture-enabled part. It evaluates element pairs even while they share a fragment ID. It differentiates a smooth combination of signed face depths, including the changing face normals, to obtain forces and separately measures geometric penetration. The legacy boundary measurement remains an additional check between distinct pieces. A padded spatial search only reuses pairs while every vertex stays inside its padded displacement bound. Contact between separate parts retains its existing formulation, with twice the penalty in fracture assemblies after the former reproducer's remaining overlap was isolated to the right support. The higher stiffness is included in the timestep bound. Cohesive opening, shear, and fracture work continue to use the existing law.

The aligned extra-fine half-step replay reaches 115 ms, forms a fourth fragment, and reaches 1.309% peak overlap with 0.0372% energy error. At fixed positions, the measured same-body and interbody contact energy changes are zero across node splitting and fragment detachment. The staggered fine fixture completes at both steps with 0.875% penetration and tagged receiver collisions. These checks cover discrete contact at the supported steps. They leave tunneling at larger steps, spatial convergence, and fracture-path convergence open.

All 12 unnotched runs now complete with at most 1.593% penetration and 0.242% energy error. The acceptance study remains nonzero. Only 8 runs reached the receiver and 4 tagged a new-face collision. At extra-fine staggered, halving the step shifts split time by 9.78% and fracture work by 12.22%. The extra-fine staggered notch also misses the matched-advance target.

The damage-disabled notched-bend control reproduces the 13.69% peak-load difference on the archived step and penalty. At a shared 0.531 µs step, the difference falls from 13.65% to 2.40% when the penalty doubles. Doubling it again lifts both absolute peaks by about 41% and leaves a 2.39% mesh difference. The first-contact peak depends strongly on the numerical contact compliance, even with no cohesive insertion. The smaller difference therefore provides no convergence claim for peak force.

### Controlled striker loading

`ImpactCheck --compliant-striker` compares the legacy penalty with a verification-only massless pad. The pad uses compression-only material-point springs over the 25 mm striker footprint. Its 4 mm thickness and 250 kPa modulus specify a combined stiffness of 1.5625 MN/m² per unit out-of-plane depth. Neither the production scene nor its contact parameters use this pad. At fixed compliance the peak reaction is 1.023–1.037 kN/m across both patterns and three refinements. Consecutive refinements change the peak and the vertical impulse through 8 ms by at most 1.1%. Halving the step has no visible effect at the reported precision, and doubling the footprint quadrature changes the peak by about 0.017%.

With the numerical penalty, doubling stiffness from ×2 to ×4 raises the peak about 41% and shortens its onset-to-peak time from about 14 to 10 µs. In a local linear impact mode with effective mass μ, stiffness k, and incoming speed v, the predicted peak scales as v√(kμ) and the rise time as √(μ/k). The vertical momentum-derived force shows the same spike, and halving the step changes the ×4 peak by less than 0.03%. This supports a spring-inertia explanation. The vertical impulse through 8 ms changes less than 0.4% over penalty ×1–×4 at matched step, while the early impulse through 4.05 ms varies substantially. These are numerical comparisons for the specified fixture.

`ImpactCheck --compliant-fracture` retains the original fracture law. On ×3, fracture insertion first occurs about 18.5–18.8 mm from the notch and precedes either support contact. On ×4 the pad peak differs by 0.20% between orientations, but the aligned crack reaches 21 mm while the staggered crack has zero notch-connected advance. Halving the staggered ×3 step changes final fracture work from 0.723751 to 0.674181 J/m. A converged early striker load is insufficient to establish fracture convergence.

In the production staggered ×3 scene, receiver impulse at 115 ms is 0.6511, 0.0675, and 0.6424 N·s/m at nominal, half, and quarter steps. The large piece first reaches the receiver at 114.06 ms in the half-step run, compared with 94.83 and 93.86 ms at the other steps. Extending only the half-step diagnostic to 155 ms recovers part of its impulse, to 0.3635 N·s/m, after the collision ends. Its remaining deficit is a trajectory difference. The acceptance window stays at 115 ms.

## Verification at the original experimental checkpoint

**Insertion passes the controlled patches.** Axial stiffness was identical at 35,998.20 N/m per unit strain. The intact weak pulse reached its gauge at 3.771 ms in both runs. The largest observed insertion change on the standard assembly was 1.78e−15 kg/m in mass, 4.44e−16 N·s/m in linear momentum, and 2.22e−16 J/m in kinetic energy. Opening, sliding, and mixed-mode patches absorbed 0.14000002 J/m against 0.14000000 prescribed. Seeded traction matched the resolved mean to roundoff. The individual bulk tractions on the two sides of a face differed by up to one interface strength in the standard impact, so the patch result establishes continuity of the seed with the discrete mean.

**Crack consistency fails.** Both standard notched meshes missed the 10.5 mm crack-advance target. At extra fine, changing pattern shifted peak load by 13.69% and work at matched advance by 54.47%. Work is interpolated between the two measured advances that bracket the target. Fine-to-extra-fine staggered exit sets differed by 4.53 mm, above the 3.02 mm tolerance. Likely contributors include the edge-restricted path cost, the different tractions on adjacent elements, and spatial sensitivity of the striker contact load.

**Secondary interaction fails across the test matrix.** Actual receiver contact occurred in 7 of 12 unnotched runs, including 4 with a tagged new target face. Only extra-fine aligned passed every timestep, energy and penetration tolerance. The standard aligned run missed the receiver and had 3.64% work sensitivity to step halving. The extra-fine staggered run had 18.08% split-time sensitivity, 7.43% work sensitivity and 2.42% penetration. The fine staggered run rejected a step at 28.771 ms when sibling contact added 0.387 J/m of penalty energy in one step, with 7.06% trial penetration. Its half-step run completed on a different fracture trajectory. The verifier reports per-piece sampled clearance, contact episodes, and scalar normal impulse. Scalar impulse sums the positive penalty loads from both contact directions.

The extra-fine aligned app run is paused at 40.20 ms. It logged a newly exposed target-face receiver contact at 39.921 ms. The native smoke check also exercised construction from blank, save and deterministic replay, striker relocation, refinement, and the comparison sheet. The headless regression suite passed. A genuine 0.7 saved scene also reopened under the new solver.

## Independent audit and insertion-stress repair (branch `claude/fracture-audit`)

**The shipped notched bend measures a competition between crack sites, not path consistency.** The striker sits 18 mm off the notch. Under it the bending moment is about 29% higher than at the notch. With the compliant pad, the first insertion on every mesh is on the bottom surface under the striker, 18.5 mm from the notch tip. Top-surface cracks during the free-flight flexural wave follow. Whether the notch crack or the striker-side crack wins changes with mesh pattern and resolution under either insertion criterion, so its notch-connected metrics cannot converge. `ImpactCheck --fracture-map <pattern> <refinement> <dt µs> <ms> 0 quiet` runs the same pad fixture with the striker centred over the notch. There the notch crack crosses the ligament on every mesh from ×3 up. That fixture is the path-consistency control.

**Defect found: the element-mean insertion stress grows spurious parallel cracks.** On the centred fixture, aligned ×3 branched at the neutral axis at 6.654 ms. Four faces were inserted in one step, two of them 3 mm from the crack at 1.15 × strength. Interior faces cannot open alone, so they stay overstressed until a connecting face qualifies, and then the whole set opens. Spent vertical crack work was 0.161, 0.271 and 0.142 J/m at ×4–×6. A single straight crack costs 21 mm × 7 J/m² = 0.147 J/m.

**Change.** Insertion now uses a linear least-squares recovery of the first Piola stress at the face endpoints. The fit runs over the incident elements of the current connectivity, so it never spans an open crack, and widens to two rings only where one ring cannot fix a linear field. It reproduces linear fields exactly, including bending stress at free surfaces, which plain nodal averaging underestimates. The weights form a fixed linear operator rebuilt only on topology change, so runtime is unchanged (standard ×1 run 0.93 s). The cohesive law, strength, toughness, contact and all thresholds are unchanged. `--legacy-insertion` restores the old criterion. The sibling- and detachment-contact replays pin it, because they replay specific 0.8 trajectories.

**Centred fixture, recovered criterion, dt 0.225 µs.** Aligned spent notch crack: 0.1470 J/m at ×4, ×5 and ×6 (the single straight crack). Staggered notch-crack work went 0.2300 → 0.2296 J/m and total work 0.3049 → 0.3045 J/m from ×5 to ×6. Crack crossing of y = 2.5 mm: aligned 8.04, 7.99, 7.69 ms and staggered 9.30, 8.93, 8.53 ms at ×4–×6. At ×5–×6 the notch-crack work differs by 12–13% between patterns. The staggered pattern has no vertical edges, so its zigzag path is at least 11.8% longer. The remaining orientation difference is that geometric floor. Halving the step at ×5 changed crossing time by 0.15% (aligned) and 0.04% (staggered), and total work by 0.7% and 2.6%. Compliant-pad energy error stayed below 0.05%.

**Limits of validity.** The Irwin length E·Gc/σ² is 7.8 mm, and convergence appears only at h ≤ 1.8 mm (×5). Production resolutions ×1–×3 (h = 9, 4.5, 3 mm) are under-resolved under either criterion. With the recovered criterion, the unnotched ×1 and ×2 scenes damage but do not split within 115 ms. The default `ImpactCheck` "Standard" gate therefore fails at ×1, and the 12-run study gives 3/12 receiver hits against 8/12 for legacy. Receiver trajectory and impulse downstream of competing nucleation sites remain chaotic at these resolutions. Crack paths remain restricted to mesh edges, and the staggered pattern carries the tortuosity penalty above.
